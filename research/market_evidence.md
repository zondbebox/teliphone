## One proposed pilot segment

**India-resident adults who are comfortable booking in English, traveling independently as couples or small friend groups, and seeking a 2-3 day guided nature trip from Almaty.** Recruit participants by these criteria; do not assume every Indian traveler speaks English or has these preferences. The 2-3 day preference, willingness to pay and demand for guided nature trips are hypotheses, not findings.

| Candidate country | Comparable observed signal | Decision reasoning and limits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| China | 127,059 accommodation guests, Jan-Sep 2024 [S01] | Largest of these three in this series. Language, discovery channels and payment needs require their own validation; this English-only prototype cannot establish fit. |
| India | 91,148 accommodation guests, same period [S01] | Meaningful observed flow; official short-visit visa information [S02] and documented Delhi/Mumbai-Almaty air links [S03] support a narrow access hypothesis. Proposed first pilot. |
| Germany | 24,672 accommodation guests, same period [S01] | A credible smaller flow. Nature interest and willingness to buy this operator's trips are unmeasured; no claim of inferior customer value. |

[S01](https://www.gov.kz/memleket/entities/tourism/press/news/details/939940?lang=ru) is a Kazakhstan Tourism Industry Committee summary citing BNS. It describes people staying in hotels and other accommodation; it does **not** isolate leisure travel, purchase intent, unique persons, English speakers or this operator's customers. Its separate border-arrival figure is not used as the product's addressable market. Country-of-origin wording is not a substitute for a verified residence/nationality definition. The count supports comparison, not a sales forecast.

The [Indian Embassy notice S02](https://www.indembastana.gov.in/page/kazakhstan-introduced-visa-free-travel-for-citizens-of-india-for-up-to-14-days/) describes up to 14 continuous visa-free days and at most 42 days in 180 for Indian citizens. Citizenship and residence differ; check each real traveler's current official requirements before launch. [Air India's 10 September 2025 announcement S03](https://www.airindia.com/content/air-india/in/en/newsroom/press-release/Air-India-and-Air-Astana-enter-codeshare-partnership.html) identifies Air Astana services between Almaty and Delhi/Mumbai, while codeshare sales were subject to approvals. This is access evidence, not a live timetable or a promise of included flights.

**Why this proposal:** it combines a visible accommodation flow and documented access with a deliberately recruitable English-language segment. It does not prove India is the best commercial market. The team should accept, change or reject it after interviewing travelers and confirming operator delivery from Almaty. Saryarqa Routes being based in Astana does not establish an Almaty transfer or departure operation; those are explicitly proposed pilot arrangements.

## Problem-to-product logic

The four complaints are case facts, not new interviews. The narrow segment adds testable questions: can participants understand meeting locations and physical demands in English, distinguish exclusions from included services, and recognize a pending payment without trying to pay twice?

| Case complaint/barrier | Proposed intervention | Evidence needed before claiming improvement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Discovery outside existing Russian-language sales | English destination pages and small, capped search campaigns leading to one matching journey | Search/query interviews; qualified visits; acquisition cost per incremental completed traveler |
| Unclear booking | Match known dates/party/budget/start city/difficulty, then select a departure | Observed unassisted task completion and valid no-match handling |
| Trust uncertainty | Visible operator identity, precise meeting instructions and dated Trip Truth Card | Comprehension and confidence results; verified operating credentials from operator |
| Inclusions unclear | One structured set of itinerary, exclusions, price and policy data throughout the journey | Participants correctly identify total and two exclusions; no cross-screen contradictions |
| Foreign-card failure | Planned hosted provider checkout with explicit pending/declined/recovery states | Approved merchant onboarding and issuer-specific test matrix; demo only today |

Proposed acquisition test: publish operator-approved English destination information, run a capped campaign aimed at adults already considering Kazakhstan, and trace visits through eligible matches, checkout starts, confirmed payments and completed trips. These steps are planned, not launched. The financial model must distinguish newly acquired completed travelers from existing customers moving online. No partner agreements, free distribution or organic growth are assumed to exist.

## Payment candidate and unresolved launch gates

**Halyk ePay is a candidate, not a completed integration or a guaranteed solution to every foreign-card decline.** Its [bank page S06](https://halykbank.kz/business/payment/epay) describes account/application/terminal onboarding and lists a 3% tariff for other-bank cards. The [provider tariff page S07](https://epayment.kz/) lists Visa/Mastercard/UPI and other card schemes. These reviewed pages do not establish a complete India-issued-card acceptance matrix. Do not infer Indian Unified Payments Interface support from the ambiguous card-scheme abbreviation UPI.

| Area | Verified documentation | Still required |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Kazakhstan merchant onboarding | Halyk account, application and terminal steps [S06] | Operator legal entity, documentation, eligible activity and written acceptance |
| Foreign-issued cards | Named international card schemes and other-bank tariff [S07] | Written supported issuer/country/BIN and 3-D Secure matrix; controlled India-issued-card tests |
| Currency | KZT appears in payment-request examples [S08] | Contractual settlement currency, timing, reserve and FX/chargeback terms; API example is not settlement proof |
| Fees | Published base rate of 3% for other-bank cards [S06/S07] | Merchant-specific quote, taxes, refund fee treatment and chargeback costs |
| Refunds | Full/partial refund of CHARGE transactions; 10 KZT minimum [S09] | Operational refund policy, customer timeframes and reconciliation test |
| Test facilities | Separate payment and status test endpoints [S08/S10] | Merchant-authorized sandbox setup and executed test log; none performed here |

[Payment-page documentation S08](https://epayment.kz/docs/platezhnaya-stranica) separates return URLs from server notifications and directs merchants to query transaction status to verify notifications. [Status documentation S10](https://epayment.kz/docs/check-status-payment) says query success is separate from payment state. [Refund documentation S09](https://epayment.kz/docs/vozvrat-chastichnyi-vozvrat) documents full and partial refund operations.

Proposed production design: keep merchant secrets server-side; compare invoice, amount, currency and terminal against the reservation; validate the provider notification as documented and reconcile it using authenticated status lookup. Use atomic inventory holds and unique payment/event identifiers so retrying a notification does not issue a second booking. Keep NEW/pending states pending and recover by reconciliation, never by trusting a browser return. These are engineering requirements, not claims that the local demo contacts Halyk.

The pilot must not accept real payments until onboarding, settlement, security, refund and India-issued-card checks pass. Obtain operational/legal advice on privacy, consent, retention and customer terms before collecting actual traveler data. No compliance certification is claimed.
